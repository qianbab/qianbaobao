# China Company Benchmarking

Use this reference when applying S_research to an industry-chain report that must compare global leaders with representative Chinese companies.

## Required Table Fields

For each value-chain segment, include:

- `segment`: upstream, midstream, downstream, enabling infrastructure, software/service, or end-market.
- `bottleneck`: what is scarce or hard to replicate.
- `global_leaders`: 3-8 representative non-Chinese companies when available.
- `china_companies`: representative mainland/Hong Kong/Taiwan-listed or private Chinese companies.
- `comparison_logic`: why these companies are comparable, and where the comparison breaks.
- `validation_signals`: orders, revenue mix, approvals, installed base, capacity, gross margin, technical milestone, customer qualification, or export/license deal.
- `risk`: commoditization, price war, policy exposure, clinical/regulatory failure, customer concentration, export controls, or balance-sheet risk.

## Benchmarking Rules

- Benchmark by function, not by market cap. A Chinese company should be placed where it competes in the chain, even if it is smaller or less profitable.
- Separate direct substitutes from adjacent exposure:
  - Direct substitute: same product, same buyer, similar performance requirements.
  - Adjacent exposure: supplier, service provider, equipment vendor, channel, or partial product overlap.
- Identify whether the Chinese company has global competitiveness, domestic substitution value, or only thematic exposure.
- Avoid treating "industry demand is large" as proof of company value. Tie each company to orders, customers, product qualification, regulatory status, capacity, or technical differentiation.
- Include private companies when they are representative, but label them as private/unlisted if relevant.
- For investment-facing work, separate industrial importance from equity attractiveness.

## Output Pattern

Use this compact format inside reports:

| Segment | Global leaders | China companies | Why comparable | What to verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example segment | Company A; Company B | Company C; Company D | Same bottleneck or customer problem | Revenue mix, orders, qualification, margin, risk |

After the table, add a short paragraph:

- `Best bottleneck exposure`: companies closest to scarce capacity or pricing power.
- `Likely commoditized exposure`: companies with weak differentiation or price-war risk.
- `Watchlist catalysts`: data readouts, approvals, capacity ramps, tender prices, export orders, customer qualification, or policy milestones.
